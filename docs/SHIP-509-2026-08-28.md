**No fourth table theme, deliberately.** `constants/visualThemes.ts` holds exactly three themes and

and those are settled and out of bounds. Inventing paint to fill the fourth slot would have been

### 3.2 Priced against the float, not intuition

Measured on real devices (harness excluded), 2026-08-28:

```
average balance   2,270        median balance   2,530
cosmetics catalogue BEFORE   1,150   =  51% of an average balance
cosmetics catalogue AFTER    2,250   =  99% of an average balance, 89% of the median
```

*(The brief's ~2,368 is close; my measured average excluding the 29 harness devices is 2,270.)*

That is the whole pricing argument. At 1,150 a typical player could own **every cosmetic in the
game twice over** out of chips they already had — owning everything cost nothing they would miss.
At 2,250 the full set costs essentially one entire balance, so completing the catalogue becomes
something a player earns. Each new item is priced above its predecessor in the same family
(150→250, 200→350, 300→500), keeping the first of each family as the cheap entry point.

### 3.4 The card back — three rules, and the cue re-measured against a control

**Neutral:** every channel is a grey. Gold means WON and mint means the field; a back borrowing
either would put one mark on two opposite states — the exact collision the original gold back was
removed for.

**No leak, by construction:** `renderBack()` is never passed the card, so nothing can vary by rank
or suit. A back is chosen by its owner and drawn identically on all their face-down cards.

**Not mistakeable for a face:** `#2E3238` sits far below the warm near-white `#FCFAF3`.

It separates from both shipped backs on **two** channels, not one — luminance
(`#18181c` < `#2E3238` < `#4A5058`) and ring count (1 / 3 / 2), because luminance alone is weak in
greyscale. Ring arithmetic checked at every card size: the third ring stays positive, minimum 6px.

**Cue re-measured — and against a control, not an expectation.** A whole-screen border census on
`/results` after a win, run three times with only the card back changed:

| back | gold | mint | neutral |
|---|---|---|---|
| classic (free) | 2px ×28, 1px ×5 | 1px ×4 | 1px ×5 |
| slate (paid, existing) | 2px ×28, 1px ×5 | 1px ×4 | 1px ×5 |
| **graphite (new)** | **2px ×28, 1px ×5** | **1px ×4** | **1px ×5** |

**Byte-identical.** Being precise about what that does and does not establish: this is a census of
every `borderTopWidth` on the screen, not a measurement of the documented gold-3 / mint-2 /
neutral-1 spec on the winning card. It establishes **invariance** — the new back changes nothing —
which is the question the brief asks. It does not re-derive the spec values.
