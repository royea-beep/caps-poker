# FIND THE OLD ICON — 2026-09-07

**Roye is right on both counts. The icon with the letter C exists, and nobody decided to replace it.**

There are in fact **two** C icons, not one. Neither was ever compared against the icon that replaced
them. Nothing in the app was changed by this sprint.

---

## §1 · Every icon CAPS has ever had

Found by walking **content, not filenames**. `git rev-list --objects --all` gave 12,531 objects;
990 of them are images; every blob that has ever lived at an icon path was extracted with
`git cat-file` and hashed. **32 blobs across 8 icon paths collapse to 21 distinct images**, because
this project overwrites icons in place and reuses one blob across `icon.png`, `adaptive-icon.png`
and the three Android layers. A filename search would have found six commits and missed that
`icon.png` has held six different pictures.

Six of those distinct images have been `app.json`'s `icon`. In order:

| # | id | commit | date | icon for | what it depicts |
|---|----|--------|------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | EXPO | `91f0f6a` | 2026-03-11 | hours | Expo template chevron on pale blue, construction guides still visible |
| 2 | FLAT | `20c853d` | 2026-03-11 | hours | flat dark green square, **no mark at all** — its own commit calls it a placeholder |
| 3 | CP | `adee655` | 2026-03-11 | 1 day | gold **CP** on dark green, small gold spade lower-right, rounded with transparent corners |
| 4 | **C-GREEN** | `582e93c` | 2026-03-12 | 6 days | **ivory letter C** on a poker-green gradient, gold double ring, four suit pips at the corners |
| 5 | **C-BLACK** | `64cef22` | 2026-03-18 | **165 days** | **gold letter C** on near-black, thin gold ring, four suit pips (gold club, red heart, red diamond, gold spade) |
| 6 | C1 | `fe1b60c` | 2026-08-30 | today | cream playing card, gold border, navy spade, near-black ground — **the current icon** |

**C-BLACK was the icon for 165 days — longer than every other icon in this list put together.**
Its commit message calls it "new 1024x1024 gold C poker icon" and it was generated by
`scripts/generate-icon.py`, which is still in the repo. C-GREEN's commit says "gold 'C' on poker
green gradient with ring border + corner suits".

All six are committed at `docs/icon-history/0*.png`. `05-C1-CARD-…png` is **byte-identical** to the
shipped `assets/icon.png` (sha256 `50e44c2f…`), so the current icon in the sheet is the real one and
not a re-render.

### Why the comparison never happened

`docs/thirty-directions/icon-legibility.json` has exactly **seven rows**: `C1, I1, J2, K3, K7, K6, K5`.
All seven were drawn from scratch for that sprint — `docs/thirty-directions/_icon-test.png` shows
them: an ace of spades, a lone spade, a card stack, and four trophy directions. **The incumbent is not
a row.** The sprint compared seven new options against each other and never against the one the
product already had. That is an omission, not a decision, exactly as Roye framed it.

## §2 · The comparison

Two artefacts, both committed:

* **`docs/icon-history/ICON-COMPARISON.png`** — 1600×5231. All six icons at **1024 / 240 / 120 / 60**,
  each with a second row **masked to a circle**, and the measurements printed **beside** the pictures.
* **`docs/icon-history/FIRST-SECOND.png`** — 1240×1766. Each of CP / C-GREEN / C-BLACK / C1 shown as
  **icon → splash → home**. The splash and home are the current ones and identical in every row; only
  the icon changes, so the strip asks one question: which icon hands off into a green-felt splash with
  a gilded serif wordmark without a jolt.

Generator: `docs/icon-history/compare.mjs` (`xvfb-run -a node docs/icon-history/compare.mjs`).
It lives under `docs/` because this sprint's edit scope is `docs/` and `tests/`, and a sheet you
cannot regenerate is a screenshot, not evidence.

### The numbers, at 60px — the home screen

| | subject share | contrast | regions ≥1% | crispness | mark outside the circle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EXPO | 13.8% | 2.08:1 | 1 | 0.272 | 0.67% |
| FLAT | 0% | 1:1 | 0 | 0 | 0% |
| CP | 99% ⚠️ | 1.68:1 | 1 | 0.116 | 21% |
| **C-GREEN** | 10.1% | 1.91:1 | 2 | 0.072 | 0% |
| **C-BLACK** | 13.8% | **4.25:1** | 1 | 0.156 | 0% |
| **C1 (current)** | **41.4%** | **18.96:1** | 1 | **0.470** | 0% |

Measured on the real chromium stamp with the maths **copied verbatim** from
`tools/thirty-directions/icon-legibility.mjs`, so C1's figures here are comparable to the ones it was
chosen on. Proof of equivalence: this rig's `C1-60.png` measures 41.4 / 18.96 / 0.470 and so does
`docs/d1-home/_icon-CAPS-60.png`, which the approval tool itself produced from `assets/icon.png` —
identical to three decimals.

The instrument's own caveat carries over unchanged: these numbers say how **big**, how **separated**
and how **crisp** a shape is. They do **not** say whether it is recognisable. A blank rectangle scores
like a playing card. **The pictures decide.**

### Two things the sheet had to state rather than hide

**C-GREEN's gold has nothing painted behind it.** 7.9% of its pixels are translucent and the
translucent ones are all the gold: the ring and the fill of the C are `rgb(255,221,102)` at
**alpha 100**, with no opaque layer underneath. Flattened on white it is bright gold, which is what
the 2026-03-12 generator showed its author. iOS composites an icon onto **black**, which gives
`rgb(100,87,40)` — a muddy brown. The sheet shows both tiles side by side under a callout. This is a
defect in the asset, not in the design, and it is the reason every source in this rig is flattened on
black **before** it is stamped, so the square and circle rows show the same colours.

**Two tiles trip the instrument's UNRELIABLE flag and for different reasons.** FLAT reads 0% subject
because it genuinely has no mark — the flag fires but the zero is true. CP reads 99% because its
rounded corners are transparent, so after flattening the corners are black while its whole green field
is not; that is a fact about the asset's corners, not a legibility score. Both are captioned on the
sheet rather than left as bare red text.

## §3 · The opinion, since you asked for one

**C-BLACK is the better mark. C1 is the better 60px tile. Those are two different questions and the
numbers only answer the second one.**

**Why C-BLACK.** It says the product's name. A gold **C** for CAPS is a mark; a playing card is the
stock photo of every card game on the store, and at 60px C1 is a white rectangle with a dot on it.
C-BLACK is also the only icon in the list whose colour already belongs to the app — the splash
wordmark is a gilded serif on green and a gold C is one step from it, while C1's cream card and navy
spade are the **FIVE-O palette the product moved away from**. `CLAUDE.md` already records the icon as
the last thing in the first second still standing on the old black-and-navy ground; the strip in
`FIRST-SECOND.png` is that sentence as a picture. And it was the icon for 165 days, and the person who
looked at it every one of those days asked for it back.

**Where C1 genuinely wins, and it is not close.** 18.96:1 contrast against 4.25:1. 41.4% of the tile
against 13.8%. Three times crisper. And C-BLACK loses a region between 1024 and 60 — **the four corner
suit pips dissolve**; look at its 60px tile, they are gone. On a bright home screen a thin gold C on
black is a dark smudge and a cream card is not. I am not putting a thumb on the scale for the
incumbent, and I am not putting one on the scale against it either.

**So neither is finished.** Every one of C-BLACK's weak numbers follows from one decision: the C is
small in a big empty frame with four ornaments that do not survive the size it is actually seen at.
Draw the C at roughly the share of the tile C1's card occupies, drop the corner pips, put the gold at
the app's own `#c9a84c` weight — and it beats C1 on contrast, on size and on crispness while keeping
the thing C1 never had, which is the name. **That is a repaint, not a restore, and this sprint
deliberately did not do it.** The brief said show it as it was, and a redraw would have been me
deciding again.

### A correction to the record, found while the file was open

`docs/thirty-directions/icon-legibility.json` records C1 at subject share **48.8%** and crispness
**0.557**. The icon that actually shipped measures **41.4%** and **0.470** — same renderer, same maths,
and this sheet's figure is identical to the approval tool's own `_icon-CAPS-60.png`. **That row was
measured on the concept direction, not on the built asset.** Contrast is essentially unchanged
(18.73 vs 18.96) so it changes no conclusion, but the file presents it as a measurement of the shipped
icon and it is not. Left as-is rather than edited, because the thirty-directions record should show
what that sprint measured; the correction belongs here.
