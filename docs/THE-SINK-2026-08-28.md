## 1. THE UNTRACKED GRANT — CLOSED

### 1.1 It is not a function. It is a column default.

I expected the grant inside `ensure_leaderboard_row()`. Its entire body is:

```sql
INSERT INTO leaderboard (device_id) VALUES (p_device_id) ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING;
```

The 2,000 comes from **`leaderboard.total_chips DEFAULT 2000`**. So this was never one function
forgetting a ledger row — **any insert into `leaderboard` mints 2,000 silently**, from any path
that exists and any path added later. Editing `ensure_leaderboard_row` would have left every other
door open.

### 1.2 So it is a trigger, and it is proven not to double-count

`trg_ledger_starting_grant` (AFTER INSERT ON leaderboard) writes a `starting_grant` row. One place,
every path, including ones written after today.

**The load-bearing detail:** `record_hand_net` writes its ledger rows *before* its
`INSERT INTO leaderboard`. So the test "this device has no ledger rows yet" cleanly separates a
genuine opening balance from a row created while settling a hand. Without it the trigger would
double-count a new player's first hand — once as settlement, once as a grant.

Verified on two fresh devices, then deleted:

| device | how the row was created | ledger rows | float | ledger | **gap** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `caps-sink-a` | plain insert, then played | `starting_grant:2000 + play_grant:80 + hand_net:0` | 2,080 | 2,080 | **0** |
| `caps-sink-b` | created *by* settlement | `play_grant:80 + hand_net:0` | 80 | 80 | **0** |

`caps-sink-b` correctly received **no** phantom grant.

### 1.3 The gap, recorded — cutoff **2026-08-28**

```
float  (sum leaderboard.total_chips) : 1,219,217
ledger (sum chip_transactions.amount):   834,724
UNTRACKED at cutoff                  :   384,493   (31.5%), across 534 rows
```

**Not backfilled.** Inventing 534 historical rows would replace a known gap with a fabricated
record. The cutoff is stored in `app_config.ledger_untracked_cutoff` and on the column comment.

### 1.4 Other unledgered writers — Rule 8 was right, there are FIVE more

| function | what it does to chips | ledger row? | has it fired? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `submit_score` | INSERT with an **absolute** `total_chips` — can overwrite a balance outright | **NO** | yes |
| `join_sit_n_go` | `total_chips - 100` (buy-in) | **NO** | **yes — 36 player rows** |
| `join_sit_n_go_solo` | `total_chips - v_buy_in` | **NO** | **yes — 6 sessions** |
| `sng_eliminate` | `+ pool × 60/30/10%` — three separate mints | **NO** | not yet |
| `check_cups` | `+ v_cup.chip_reward` | **NO** | not yet |
| `get_poker_shop` | bare `INSERT INTO leaderboard` → mints 2,000 by default | **NO** → now covered by the trigger | yes |

These are **not fixed here** — the brief asked me to check and report, and each needs its own care.
The trigger covers only the *insert* path; the five UPDATE paths above still move chips without a
record.

### 1.5 How I would verify agreement in a week

```sql
-- devices created after the cutoff must reconcile EXACTLY
SELECT count(*) FILTER (WHERE gap <> 0) AS drifting, sum(abs(gap)) AS total_drift
FROM (
  SELECT l.device_id,
         l.total_chips - COALESCE((SELECT sum(amount) FROM chip_transactions t
                                    WHERE t.device_id = l.device_id), 0) AS gap
  FROM leaderboard l
  WHERE EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM chip_transactions t
                 WHERE t.device_id = l.device_id
                   AND t.event_type = 'starting_grant')
) x;
```

**Expected in a week: `drifting = 0` for devices that only ever played hands and claimed dailies.**
It will be **non-zero** the moment anyone joins a Sit & Go or `submit_score` absolute-writes —
which is the check *working*, not failing. Any drift it reports maps to one of the five writers in
§1.4, so the query doubles as the punch-list for the next sprint.

**Correcting the brief's expectation:** float and ledger will *not* agree for everything created
from now on. They will agree for the paths that go through the ledgered writers. Closing the
remaining five is what makes the statement true.

### 2.4 The tournament already exists — and as built it is a FAUCET, not a sink

The brief lists "the tournament entry that does not exist yet". It exists, it has run, and the
arithmetic goes the wrong way:

- `join_sit_n_go_solo` charges the real player **100** and sets `prize_pool = buy_in × 6 = 600`.
  **The five bots contribute nothing.**
- `sng_eliminate` pays **60% + 30% + 10% = 100% of the pool**.
- Measured: **6 sessions, each `buy_in 100, prize_pool 600, 6 players`** — 30 of the 36 player rows
  are bots.

So a completed solo tournament would mint **500 chips out of nothing**, with **no ledger row on
either side**. It has never actually paid out because all six sessions are stuck in
`status = 'playing'` — nothing has ever been eliminated. The leak is latent, not yet realised.

A tournament can only be a sink if the pool is built from **real** buy-ins and **less than 100%**
is paid out. Neither is true today.

### 2.5 Which candidates punish the engaged player

**A, B and F.**

- **The rake (A, B) taxes exactly the people who play most**, which is the precise opposite of what
  PLAY-NOT-PRESENCE just set out to do. It removes **0 from an opener** and 784/week from a
  regular. A sprint that moved the faucet toward players, followed by a sink aimed only at players,
  nets out to having done very little.
- **The rebuy (F) taxes the player who is losing** — the one already having the worst session.
- C, D and E are chosen, not taxed. The player decides to spend.

### 2.6 THE BINDING CONSTRAINT — the finding that matters more than the ranking

> **A player who never plays still mints 2,855 chips a week, and no sink can touch them.**

Sinks only reach people who play. So:

| presence multiplier | a regular earns/week | 15% rake removes | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.40 (today) | 8,455 | 784 | **+7,671** |
| 0.20 | 7,235 | 784 | +6,451 |
| 0.10 | 6,625 | 784 | +5,841 |
| **0.00** | 6,015 | 784 | **+5,231** |

Even with the presence grant **switched off entirely**, a regular player still nets +5,231/week.
And the play-grant cap needed for a 15% rake to hold a regular flat is:

```
required cap = (784 − 2,855) / 7 = −296     ← negative. Impossible.
```

**No sink of a defensible size holds the float flat at the current faucet.** That is not an
argument against a sink; it is the reason a sink alone was never going to be the answer.

## 3. RECOMMENDATION

### Build **C — the tournament, with a real rake on a real pool.** One candidate, and it is not the strongest number.

**⚠️ Stated here, beside the recommendation and not in a footnote: 26 devices have ever played a
hand, and 9 appear in `hand_history`. No sink value in this document can be validated. The
percentages above are arithmetic on a measured faucet, not observed behaviour. Anything below is a
product argument, not an empirical result.**

**Why C, in product terms:**

1. **It is the only candidate that creates a reason to play rather than a tax on playing.** A
   tournament entry is a thing a player *chooses* to spend on, and what they buy is a better game —
   stakes, a field, a finish. The rake and the rebuy are both charges levied on someone for the
   crime of being engaged.
2. **Most of it is already built.** Sessions, seats, elimination, prize distribution and a UI
   entry all exist. What is missing is one line of arithmetic: pay out less than 100% of the pool,
   and build the pool from real buy-ins.
3. **It fixes a latent faucet at the same time.** §2.4 is a 500-chip-per-session mint waiting to
   fire the first time a session completes. Fixing the pool to make it a sink also removes that.
4. **It scales with the population.** Cosmetics are one-off, the rake is a percentage of a small
   number, but a tournament economy grows as the player base grows — which is the only one of these
   that is still useful at 26,000 players.

**Not chosen, and why:** the rake (B) is the biggest single number and it is the wrong shape —
see §2.5. Rotating cosmetics (E) is the second-best *shape* and a reasonable second pick, but it is
an ongoing art commitment for 3.5% of the flow. Cosmetics as they stand (D) are a rounding error:
1,150 chips once in a player's lifetime.

**And the thing I would say to Roye before he picks:** the sink is the second lever, not the
first. `play_grant_daily_cap` (800) and `presence_grant_multiplier` (0.4) are already config, and
they move the float by an order of magnitude more than any sink here. If the goal is for chips to
feel scarce, the cap is the dial. The sink is what gives a player something to *want* — which is a
different and equally necessary job, and it is why C is worth building even though its number is
small.

**Nothing was implemented. No sink exists in the code after this sprint.**

## 4. THE CAVEATS THAT TRAVEL WITH IT

1. **26 devices have ever played a hand.** No value here can be validated — stated in §3 beside the
   recommendation, not relegated.
2. **The 1,219,217 already outstanding do not move. Any sink affects flow, not stock, and I am not
   proposing a reset.**

   **What that means for the first tester cohort, plainly:** they will meet a currency that already
   has no scarcity. The average existing balance is **2,283 chips** against a 75–100 buy-in — about
   25 hands of stock before anyone earns anything. A new tester starting today gets 2,000 by
   default and immediately earns 2,855/week for doing nothing. **No sink shipped now will make
   chips feel scarce to that cohort.** The first honest test of any economy change is a *fresh*
   cohort on a fresh curve — which is an argument for shipping the sink *before* a wider tester
   push, not after, and for measuring it on new devices only.
3. **Everything stays config-driven.** Any sink built from this document must put its rate in
   `app_config` and read it fresh per call, exactly as `presence_grant_multiplier`,
   `play_grant_per_hand`, `play_grant_practice_pct` and `play_grant_daily_cap` do — so the first
   real cohort can retune it without a deploy.
